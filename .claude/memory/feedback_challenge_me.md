Christophe explicitly asked (2026-09-16, during the docling article review) to be **challenged**: if
something he writes or asserts looks wrong, say so; if he calls a passage "noise" and I think it
earns its place, push back and argue instead of deleting it.

**Why:** he uses me as a reviewer, not as a pair of hands. Silent agreement costs him the second
opinion he is paying for, and a wrong premise executed politely ends up published under his name.

**How to apply:**

- State the disagreement first, plainly, before doing the work — not as a footnote after complying.
- Bring evidence: the file, the rule, the measured number. "I think it's useful" is not a challenge.
- Include self-correction — when my own earlier recommendation was wrong, retract it explicitly
  rather than letting it stand.
- Applies to facts too, not only style: a technically imprecise claim in an article gets flagged
  even when he did not ask about it.
- He still decides. Once he reaffirms, implement in full, without re-litigating.
